Our story

Not every great story begins at the top.

Kudos was struggling when it changed hands. The obvious move was to close it, rename it and relaunch. We decided to rebuild while keeping the doors open — one piece at a time, in front of the guests who kept walking in.

  1. The Takeover

    The restaurant was struggling when it changed hands. The easy move was to shut it, change the name and relaunch with a fanfare. We kept the name, kept the doors open, and got to work.

  2. The People

    People first. Chefs, captains, service professionals — the team that turns up every night is the restaurant. So that is where the rebuild began.

  3. The Kitchen

    Then the kitchen. Ingredients, prep, consistency, hygiene — all of it written down as standard procedure, so the dish you loved last month tastes the same tonight.

  4. The Rhythm

    Then the rhythm: how a service flows, how a table is read, how an afternoon turns into an evening. There was no overnight transformation. No dramatic announcement. No shortcut to getting things right.

  5. The Next Chapter

    We chose evolution over disruption. Next up is the atmosphere itself — the music, the light, the energy of the room. Kudos is not just a restaurant that changed. It is a restaurant that continues to evolve.

“The best compliment a restaurant can receive is not just a good review. It is seeing the same guests walk through its doors again.”

Vathsaal Jogani, Founder

What makes Kudos different

Eight things we refuse to get wrong.

  1. The view does half the work

    Lake Pichola and City Palace, from a rooftop in the middle of Udaipur.

  2. One table, many cuisines

    Rajasthani, North Indian, Italian, Mexican, Pan-Asian. Nobody has to agree on one kind of food.

  3. Food that does the talking

    No complicated terminology. You will remember what you ordered — and come back for it.

  4. Five non-negotiables

    Taste, presentation, ingredients, hygiene, hospitality. Every plate, every night.

  5. Written down, not winged

    Kitchen standards on paper, so the second visit tastes like the first.

  6. People, not transactions

    A team rebuilt around genuine hospitality. Not too formal, not too casual.

  7. An evening in three moods

    Golden hour, the evening, the night. The room changes with the light — stay for all three.

  8. Music with a point of view

    Saturday Socials and the Purani Playlist, set to the mood of the room.
